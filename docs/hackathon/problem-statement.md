**Markets are the most-watched thing in crypto, and the hardest to try.**

Think about what a curious newcomer has to do before their first trade: install a wallet, find gas, learn order types, read a candlestick chart, and understand leverage and liquidation. Every one of those steps is a place to give up. By the time the trade button appears, most people have already left.

We see three problems behind that.

**1. The interface is the barrier.**
Trading terminals are built for people who already read charts. A beginner gets dense screens, jargon and a dozen decisions before anything happens. The real question is simpler: *why should a new user need a trading chart at all?*

**2. Phones make it worse.**
Newcomers live on a phone, often held in one hand. Small targets and stacked confirmations either bury the main action or make a mis-tap expensive. Nobody designed the first trade for a thumb.

**3. Fun crypto apps are hard to trust.**
Games built on top of prices usually hide how a result is produced. An opaque random number or an unverifiable price turns an outcome into a claim instead of a fact. The same apps often reward chasing losses, because that keeps people playing.

**What is missing** is a first step into markets that is gentle enough for a beginner, honest enough to verify, and fast enough to feel like a game: no chart literacy, no wallet wall, no gas, and a result you can check yourself.
